# 0.9.6: what changes for players

> **Purpose:** the changes of 0.9.6 a player sees, with where each is described · **Status:** current (2026-11-11) · **Tags:** none

- Empty or broken movie pictures detected by the port now show black and finish instead of staying green ([movie handling](../engines/lux/bink.md#empty-pictures-and-failed-opens-issue-26)); Swap Force's reported treasure freeze still needs a play check.
- A movie no longer turns into a plain green picture that never ends when a read of its data fails for a moment (Swap Force with scale 4 and HD textures on Windows): the read is tried again, and the log tells when it failed ([movie data](../engines/lux/bink.md#movie-data-that-stops-arriving-issue-26)).
- Swapping figures from the Toy Box window, in-game Toy Box or quick wheel keeps the portal filled, including both swapper halves, so the swap no longer causes a request for a Skylander and reopens the Toy Box ([Toy Box](../runtime/host/toybox_ux.md#swapping-without-an-empty-portal-096-issue-40); play check pending).
- The wider view follows the game window: resizing it, maximising it or going fullscreen on a wide display widens or narrows the view at once, no restart; on a wide display the game is ready for its shape from the start, so a 16:9 window maximised there is as sharp as a game started wide (Giants and `original_resolution` excepted: a little softer until the next start) ([ultrawide](../runtime/gpu/ultrawide.md#following-the-window-issue-14-v-2026-11-12), issue #14).
- Trap Team accepts your separate voice choice when that speech is on the disc, and its in-game Commands page offers LB for keyboard and controller binding ([settings](../getting-started/settings.md); play check and standalone launcher update pending, issue #51).

## FSR 3: less trail behind a fast-moving character
- With FSR 3 upscaling or frame generation on, a moving character, enemy or prop now gives FSR its own motion instead
  of the camera's, and the place it just left is redrawn fresh: the light trail behind a running Skylander is shorter
  (Giants: about 30 % less difference where the hero was, the rest of the picture unchanged, about 0.05 ms of GPU time a
  frame). Spyro's Adventure gets only the second part (its characters' motion is not in their draws), and particles
  still smear in a fast attack ([upscaling.md](../runtime/gpu/upscaling.md#per-object-motion-096-issue-56)).

## Frame pacing: "Drop to 30 FPS when needed" and VSync on fast displays
- **The drop to 30 waits until the game really cannot hold 60.** It used to drop on a few late frames (a load, a shader
  being built, two busy seconds) and come back at once, a visible stutter each time. Now it drops only after 5 seconds
  with a quarter of the frames late, never during a load or a movie, stays at 30 for at least 30 seconds, and comes back
  only when the frames fit with a margin ([video_vblank.md](../runtime/xbox360/video_vblank.md)).
- **Off by default on dedicated graphics cards**, on for integrated graphics and Macs. Only for players who never changed
  it: the game sets it at its first start, and the launcher's Video tab and the F3 menu show it. A value you chose stays
  ([settings.md](../getting-started/settings.md)).
- **VSync on 75, 100, 144 or 165 Hz displays**: the frames no longer queue behind the display, which could slow the game
  down; each frame is shown at the next refresh, without tearing (Vulkan and Direct3D 12). 60, 120 and 240 Hz keep
  standard VSync. A 60 FPS game on a 144 Hz display still shows each frame for 2 or 3 refreshes: for perfectly even
  motion, set the display to 120 Hz or use G-Sync / FreeSync. The log says which mode is used and how long the frames
  stay on screen ([plume_backends.md](../runtime/gpu/plume_backends.md)).
- The Windows check: [test_096_pacing.md](../release/test_096_pacing.md).

## Spyro's Adventure: no more black flashes when a level first loads or something breaks
- Right after starting a new game and when the hub first loaded, a frame could show black or white shapes, a black sky
  or a scene without its colours (two players' videos on Windows, AMD and NVIDIA, Direct3D 12 and Vulkan; seen on a Mac
  too). While a level fades in, the game prepares many small batches of drawing commands, and after each one the port lost
  the next object's drawing settings, so see-through clouds and effects were drawn solid for that frame. Every backend,
  resolution scale and frame rate is concerned ([ring_mirror.md](../runtime/gpu/ring_mirror.md)).
- The same happened whenever many objects appear at once: in the first level, the bridge, or a plank of it, turned
  black for a frame when the rock broke it.

## Spyro's Adventure: no more bright blocks along the grass edges
- While the camera moved, grass in the shade could show bright yellow-green squares in a staircase along its edge (a
  player's video on Windows; seen on a Mac too). Each square is one of the sun's 32-pixel light tiles, lit without its
  shadow because the game's own job that sorts the tiles ran far too slowly: it pauses for an instant after each tile,
  and the port made each pause a real sleep. The job now runs at full speed (about 3 ms instead of 115 ms on a Mac, and
  more on Windows), so the shadows follow the camera again. Every backend and resolution scale is concerned, and only
  Spyro's Adventure has this job ([render_bugs.md](../runtime/gpu/render_bugs.md),
  [threads_sync.md](../runtime/xbox360/threads_sync.md#yielding-and-idle-loops-lux)).

## SuperChargers: your figures stay yours from one session to the next
- SuperChargers counts in its Collection (pause menu, Portal Master > Collection) only the figures registered to you. It
  used to give you a new identity at every start, so the figures you had used before became someone else's: their page
  in My Team asked you to take ownership again. It now keeps the identity stored in your save
  ([figures.md](../runtime/host/figures.md), "Owner").
- A figure registered on a console, or in Swap Force or Imaginators, still belongs to someone else for SuperChargers: it
  plays normally but is not counted. To add it, open the pause menu, My Team, choose the figure, then **Ownership**, and
  answer Yes. This writes your ownership on the figure; the game does not do it by itself.
- The game log now names the owner of each figure the game reads (`[portal] slot 0's toy: owner ...`).
## Imaginators: your own Imaginators' portraits in the HUD
- The round picture at the bottom left was plain black for every Imaginator you created (the Senseis' showed). The
  game draws that picture from your character when it comes on the portal, and the port lost it on the way. It now shows
  your Imaginator's head, with every graphics option and with or without HD textures
  ([research.md](../../games/imaginators/docs/research.md#the-created-characters-portrait-47-2026-11-11-v)).

## Xbox portals
- **Xbox 360 portals read figures** (expected; not yet tried on a portal). Their answers were all dropped: the game asked
  the portal for 64 bytes at a time while it sends 32, so each read ended in a timeout and the answer inside was thrown
  away (issues #20 and #23, on Linux; the Mac had a milder form). Each read now asks for one packet. After opening the
  portal the game also makes the start request Linux's own Xbox driver makes of such devices.
- **Windows, Xbox One portal**: reached through Windows' own Xbox driver (Windows.Gaming.Input.Custom), nothing to install
  (expected; not yet tried on a PC).
- **Windows, Xbox 360 portal**: works without libusb once it is on Windows' own WinUSB driver, set once in Device Manager
  (the launcher's README, "Xbox 360 portal on Windows"; Test says when it is needed).
- **Windows, a Wii, PlayStation or PC portal moved to WinUSB** (by Zadig, as some emulators ask): the game uses it as it
  is, through WinUSB (expected; not yet tried on a PC). On **libusbK or libusb0** (drivers Arkeyan does not ship), Test
  shows the way back as steps, in the ten languages: Device Manager > Uninstall device with "Attempt to remove the driver
  for this device", replug, and if Windows puts that driver back, `pnputil /enum-drivers` then `pnputil /delete-driver
  oemNN.inf /uninstall /force` with the package's name filled in; **Copy commands** copies both. A Trap Team Wii portal
  on libusb0 came back after a plain uninstall (0.9.5).
- **No libusb line on Windows**: Test never says "libusb-1.0 not found (set SKY_LIBUSB ...)" there (Xbox portals use
  WinUSB and Windows' Xbox driver). On Linux it reads "Xbox portals need libusb-1.0: install your system's libusb-1.0
  package, then test again."
- **Issue #23's portal answers**: its Test showed a status report (`0B 14 53 ...`) and, by its size, the 'R' answer joined
  behind it in one 64-byte read; one-packet reads now pass over the status and match the answer (a fake portal replays
  the player's packets). Test also gives each packet's time after the command and the kernel drivers after the claim.
- **Diagnostics**: Test (Copy) and the game log show an Xbox portal's interfaces, its driver, every step of opening it and
  its first packets each way, so one log tells what is still wrong.
- Details: [portal.md](../runtime/host/portal.md) ("An Xbox 360 portal that takes commands and never answers", "Xbox
  portals on Windows without libusb", "Real portals on Windows: the driver"); the owner's test: [test_096_xbox.md](../release/test_096_xbox.md).
- **The portal test on Windows**: its copied result no longer ends in unreadable characters with a Wii, PlayStation
  or PC portal, and no longer reports an error for a write method the portal doesn't need
  ([portal_probe.md](../runtime/host/portal_probe.md), issue #41).

## Windows: a black screen in Direct3D 12 switches the game to Vulkan
- When Direct3D 12 (the default) brings no picture to the screen in a game's first seconds (its presents fail, the GPU
  never finishes a frame, or the device is removed early), the game switches itself to Vulkan for good and starts again
  once: `[<game>.video] gpu_backend = vulkan`, with `gpu_backend_note` saying why. The launcher shows it on the Graphics
  API row ("Vulkan, since Direct3D 12 showed nothing on this PC.") and a toast while the game restarts; choosing Direct3D
  12 again there clears the note. A game started on its own starts its new copy itself. Forcing a backend with
  `SKY_GPU_BACKEND` turns the guard off. A picture that the GPU finishes but draws black is not caught.
  Details: [plume_backends.md](../runtime/gpu/plume_backends.md#when-d3d12-shows-nothing-vulkan-instead--2026-11-11-issue-27).
- To check on a PC: `SKY_GPU_TEST_NO_FRAME=fail` (or `stuck`, `lost`) with D3D12 chosen; the game must come back in
  Vulkan within about 10 s, the note written, `skylauncher-game.prev.log` naming the reason.
## The launcher's Windows build runs its tests
- The Windows build of the launcher already ran `ArkeyanLauncherTest --no-disc` (it caught a folder name with ':' before
  0.9.5 shipped); it is now a step of its own, with every folder and settings file in the runner's scratch folder, no
  update check and 20 minutes at most, so a failure names the step and never touches the runner's own profile.
