# 0.9.5: what changes for players

> **Purpose:** the changes of 0.9.5 a player sees, with where each is described · **Status:** current (2026-11-10) · **Tags:** none

## Windows: Direct3D 12 or Vulkan, per game
- Each game's settings (Video tab, Display section, Windows only) choose the graphics API: **Direct3D 12** (the default)
  or **Vulkan**; the F3 menu has the same row (from the next start). If the chosen one cannot start, the game starts the
  other and says why in its log. Before 0.9.5 Vulkan came first. Details: [windows.md](../getting-started/platforms/windows.md#gpu-backend),
  [settings.md](../getting-started/settings.md) (`gpu_backend`).
- Direct3D 12 now recovers from a failed present as Vulkan does (a new swap chain), and reports a lost device instead of
  retrying ([plume_backends.md](../runtime/gpu/plume_backends.md)).

## Trap Team on NVIDIA: the title logo's white glow
- The passes that read the screen at each pixel's own position landed exactly on the edge between two texels; NVIDIA
  took the one to the left and above, so every such pass moved the picture by a pixel and the logo's edge got a dark
  line. They now read inside the texel on every GPU (each game translates its shaders once more at its first start).
  Whether this brings the whole white glow back is the PC's check
  ([status.md](../../games/trapteam/docs/status.md#the-title-logos-black-outline-on-a-windows-pc-2026-11-10)).
- A draw that leaves one of its pictures unwritten now tells the GPU to keep it, as the console does (not the glow's
  cause, as first thought).
- Direct3D 12 now gives each of a draw's pictures its own blending and channels, as Vulkan and Metal do (Swap Force's
  decals wrote a channel they leave alone on the console) ([plume_backends.md](../runtime/gpu/plume_backends.md)).
- The frame dump can scan every target's numbers (`SKY_DUMP_FLOATS`), so a tester sends one log instead of 400 MB of
  pictures ([debugging.md](../porting/debugging.md)).

## The game's sound stays off a portal's speaker
- A Traptanium portal with a speaker can be an audio device, and Windows makes the Xbox One portal the default output when
  it is plugged in. The game and the launcher's music never play there: they keep the default output unless it is a
  portal's, take another output then, and follow a later change of the default. The log names the output chosen and the
  one skipped ([audio_xma_xaudio.md](../runtime/xbox360/audio_xma_xaudio.md), "The output device").

## Portals: the Xbox One portal, names by model
- The Xbox One portal (`1430:09AA`, Windows' "Xbox Gaming Device") is known: macOS and Linux use it; on Windows the
  portal test names it and says Xbox One portals come in the next version. Any other Activision device with an Xbox 360
  or an Xbox One interface counts as a portal, and an unknown one is named in the log.
- On Windows the portal test and the game log list every device that may be a portal, with its driver.
- Wired portals are named by model, not console: a Wii Trap Team portal is a "Traptanium portal" (it was called the Xbox
  360 one). Details: [portal.md](../runtime/host/portal.md) ("The portal's identity", "Xbox portals by what they are").

## The figure catalogue: what the later games define
- The catalogue reads SuperChargers' and Imaginators' own toy tables: 26 more variants (Trap Team's Dark Wildfire, Snap
  Shot and Food Fight, its Legendary and Nitro figures, Hog Wild Fryno, Heartbreaker Buckshot, the "Instant" editions),
  SuperChargers' 13 racing villains and their 15 vehicles, and two ids corrected (Sure Shot Shroomboom, Tidal Wave Gill
  Grunt). 311 toys and 420 variants; what stays uncertain:
  [collection.md](../runtime/host/collection.md) ("SuperChargers and Imaginators").
